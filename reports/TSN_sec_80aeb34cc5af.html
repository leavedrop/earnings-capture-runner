OUTLOOK
For fiscal 2027, the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) indicates domestic protein production (beef, pork, chicken and turkey) will be flat to a slight increase compared to fiscal 2026 levels. The following is a summary of the outlook for each of our segments, as well as an outlook for corporate expenses and amortization, revenue, capital expenditures, net interest expense, liquidity, free cash flow, tax rate and dividends for fiscal 2027. As our accounting cycle resulted in a 53-week year in fiscal 2026 as compared to a 52-week year in fiscal 2027, percentage changes presented below for fiscal 2027, other than USDA projections, are shown in comparison to fiscal 2026 results as adjusted to a comparable 52-week basis. Certain of the outlook numbers include adjusted operating income (loss) and segment operating income (loss), as adjusted, and free cash flow which are non-GAAP measures. The Company is not able to reconcile its full-year fiscal 2027 projected adjusted results to its fiscal 2027 projected GAAP results because certain information necessary to calculate such measures on a GAAP basis is unavailable or dependent on the timing of future events outside of our control. Therefore, because of the uncertainty and variability of the nature of and the amount of any potential applicable future adjustments, which could be significant, the Company is unable to provide a reconciliation for these forward-looking non-GAAP measures without unreasonable effort. Adjusted operating income (loss), segment operating income (loss), as adjusted, and free cash flow should not be considered substitutes for operating income (loss), cash provided by operating activities or any other measures of financial performance reported in accordance with GAAP. Investors should rely primarily on the Company’s GAAP results and use non-GAAP financial measures only supplementally in making investment decisions.
Beef
USDA projects domestic production will decrease approximately 2% in fiscal 2027 as compared to fiscal 2026. We anticipate segment operating income (loss), as adjusted, of $(300) million to $100 million in fiscal 2027.
Pork
USDA projects domestic production will be relatively flat in fiscal 2027 as compared to fiscal 2026. We anticipate segment operating income, as adjusted, of $75 million to $175 million in fiscal 2027.
Chicken
USDA projects chicken production will increase approximately 1% in fiscal 2027 as compared to fiscal 2026. We anticipate segment operating income, as adjusted, of $1.3 billion to $1.6 billion in fiscal 2027.
Prepared Foods
We anticipate segment operating income, as adjusted, of $1.25 billion to $1.4 billion in fiscal 2027.
International
We anticipate segment operating income, as adjusted, of $125 million to $200 million in fiscal 2027.
Corporate Expenses and Amortization
We anticipate corporate expenses and amortization, as adjusted, of approximately $1.0 billion in fiscal 2027.
Total Company
We anticipate total company adjusted operating income of $1.7 billion to $2.15 billion for fiscal 2027.
Revenue
We expect sales to decrease 3.5% to 5.5% in fiscal 2027 as compared to fiscal 2026, primarily due to lower Beef segment volumes resulting from our network restructuring and tight cattle supplies.
Capital Expenditures
We expect capital expenditures between $1.1 billion to $1.4 billion in fiscal 2027. Capital expenditures include investments in profit improvement projects as well as projects for maintenance and repairs.
Net Interest Expense
We expect net interest expense to approximate $360 million in fiscal 2027.
Liquidity
We expect total liquidity, which was $4.1 billion as of October 3, 2026, to remain well above our minimum liquidity target of $1.0 billion.
Free Cash Flow
We expect free cash flow to be between $0.8 billion and $1.3 billion for fiscal 2027.
Tax Rate
We currently expect our adjusted effective tax rate to approximate 25% in fiscal 2027.
Dividends
Effective November 13, 2026, the Board of Directors increased the quarterly dividend previously declared on August 6, 2026, to $0.52 per share on our Class A common stock and $0.468 per share on our Class B common stock. The increased quarterly dividend is payable on December 15, 2026, to shareholders of record at the close of business on December 1, 2026. The Board also declared on November 13, 2026, a quarterly dividend of $0.52 per share on our Class A common stock and $0.468 per share on our Class B common stock, payable on March 15, 2027, to shareholders of record at the close of business on March 1, 2027. We anticipate the remaining quarterly dividends in fiscal 2027 will be $0.52 and $0.468 per share of our Class A and Class B common stock, respectively. This results in an annual dividend rate in fiscal 2027 of $2.08 for Class A shares and $1.872 for Class B shares, or a 2% increase compared to the fiscal 2026 annual dividend rate.

Non-GAAP Financial Measures
Adjusted Sales (due to the impact of an additional week in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026), Adjusted Operating Income (Loss), Adjusted Income before Income Taxes, Adjusted Income Tax Expense, Adjusted Net Income Attributable to Tyson and Adjusted EPS, EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, net debt to EBITDA, net debt to Adjusted EBITDA, Segment Operating Income (Loss), As Adjusted, Corporate Expenses, As Adjusted, Amortization, As Adjusted, and Free Cash Flow are presented as supplemental financial measures in the evaluation of our business that are not required by, or presented in accordance with GAAP. The non-GAAP financial measures are tools intended to assist our management and investors in comparing our performance on a consistent basis for purposes of business decision-making by removing the impact of certain items that management believes do not directly reflect our core operations on an ongoing basis. These non-GAAP measures should not be a substitute for their comparable GAAP financial measures. Investors should rely primarily on our GAAP results and use non-GAAP financial measures only supplementally in making investment decisions. We believe the presentation of these non-GAAP financial measures helps management and investors to assess our operating performance from period to period, including our ability to generate earnings sufficient to service our debt, enhances understanding of our financial performance and highlights operational trends. These measures are widely used by investors and rating agencies in the valuation, comparison, rating and investment recommendations of companies. Our calculation of non-GAAP measures may not be comparable to similarly titled measures reported by other companies and other companies may not define these non-GAAP financial measures in the same way, which may limit their usefulness as comparative measures.
Definitions
EBITDA is defined as net income (loss) before interest, income taxes, depreciation and amortization. Net debt to EBITDA (Adjusted EBITDA) represents the ratio of our debt, net of cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments, to EBITDA (and to Adjusted EBITDA). EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, net debt to EBITDA and net debt to Adjusted EBITDA are presented as supplemental financial measurements in the evaluation of our business.
Adjusted EBITDA, Adjusted Operating Income (Loss), Adjusted Income before Income Taxes, Adjusted Income Tax Expense, Adjusted Net Income Attributable to Tyson and Adjusted EPS are defined as EBITDA, Operating Income (Loss), Income (Loss) before Income Taxes, Income Tax Expense (Benefit), Net Income (Loss) Attributable to Tyson and diluted earnings per share, respectively, excluding the impacts of any items that management believes do not directly reflect our core operations on an ongoing basis, as well as the impact of an additional week in fiscal 2026.
Adjusted Sales is defined as reported Sales excluding the impact of the additional week in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026. The impact of the additional week is calculated by dividing the fourth quarter reported Sales, excluding legal contingency accruals recognized as a reduction to Sales that were not impacted by the additional week, by 14 weeks. We believe Adjusted Sales is a useful measure as it improves period-to-period comparability of sales when a fiscal year includes a 53-week accounting cycle.
Segment Operating Income (Loss) is defined as Operating Income (Loss) excluding corporate expenses and amortization. Corporate expenses are unallocated general and administrative costs, including the costs of corporate functions, that are shared across multiple segments. Amortization includes amortization from intangible assets, including brands and trademarks, customer relationships, supply arrangements, patents and intellectual property and land use rights, as well as amortization of software.
Segment Operating Income (Loss), As Adjusted is defined as Segment Operating Income (Loss) excluding items affecting comparability, which in management’s judgment, affect the period-to-period assessment of operating results. Items affecting comparability include restructuring and related charges (including network optimization), plant closure and disposal charges (net of gains), goodwill and intangible impairments, brand and product line discontinuations, facility fire related costs (net of insurance proceeds) and certain non-ordinary course legal, regulatory and other matters, as well as the impact of an additional week in fiscal 2026.
Corporate Expenses, As Adjusted is defined as Corporate Expenses excluding items affecting comparability, which in management’s judgment, affect the period-to-period assessment of operating results. Items affecting comparability include restructuring and related charges (including network optimization), corporate asset disposal charges (net of gains) and certain non-ordinary course legal, regulatory and other matters, as well as the impact of an additional week in fiscal 2026.
Amortization, As Adjusted is defined as Amortization excluding the impact of items affecting comparability, which in management’s judgment, affect the period-to-period assessment of operating results. Items affecting comparability include accelerated amortization related to the discontinuance of intangible assets, as well as the impact of an additional week in fiscal 2026.
Free Cash Flow is defined as Cash Provided by Operating Activities minus payments for Property, Plant and Equipment.

About Tyson Foods, Inc.
Tyson Foods, Inc. (NYSE: TSN) is a world-class food company and recognized leader in protein. Founded in 1935 by John W. Tyson, it has grown under four generations of family leadership. The Company is guided by its core values and unified by this purpose: Tyson Foods. We Feed the World Like Family™ and has a broad portfolio of iconic products and brands including Tyson®, Jimmy Dean®, Hillshire Farm®, Ball Park®, Wright®, State Fair®, aidells® and ibp®. Tyson Foods is dedicated to bringing high-quality food to every table in the world, safely and affordably, now and for future generations. Headquartered in Springdale, Arkansas, the Company is a member of the S&P 500 and Russell 1000 large capitalization indices. It had approximately 124,000 team members on October 3, 2026. Visit www.tysonfoods.com.
