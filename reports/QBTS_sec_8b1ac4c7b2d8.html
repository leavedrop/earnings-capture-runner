D-Wave Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results
Third Quarter Revenue Up 21% Year Over Year
Remaining Performance Obligations of $38.3 Million Up 1,221% Year Over Year
PALO ALTO, Calif. – November 10, 2026 – D-Wave Quantum Inc. (NASDAQ: QBTS) (“D-Wave” or the “Company”), the only dual-platform quantum computing company, providing both annealing and gate-model systems, software, and services, today announced financial results for its third quarter ended September 30, 2026.
“From Seoul to Como to Washington, we are seeing growing interest in D-Wave’s differentiated dual-platform approach and a determination to put our quantum computing technology to work today,” said Dr. Alan Baratz, CEO of D-Wave. “Our triple-digit increase in year-to-date bookings and robust pipeline reinforce our confidence in the opportunity ahead. We are expanding our commercial reach and executing on both our annealing and gate-model roadmaps with a clear objective: to lead this industry in technical and scientific innovation, measurable customer value and growth.”
Recent Business and Technical Highlights
•Revenue for the third quarter of 2026 was $4.5 million, up 21% year over year.
•Bookings for the first nine months of 2026 were $37.4 million, a 611% year over year increase.
•Executed a definitive agreement with the U.S. Department of Commerce, following the previously announced letter of intent, providing D-Wave with access to up to $100 million of U.S. CHIPS and Science Act funding, in installments upon satisfying specified milestones and conditions. The funding is intended to support development of D-Wave’s next-generation quantum computers, including a 100,000-qubit annealing quantum computer targeting greater performance across optimization, materials simulation, blockchain and artificial intelligence. It is also intended to support development of a 10,000-qubit gate-model quantum computer designed to enable 100 logical qubits capable of reliably executing more than one million operations, enabling applications in areas such as quantum chemistry and quantum AI.
•Announced that NTT DOCOMO, Japan’s largest mobile operator, deployed its second production application powered by D-Wave’s hybrid quantum technology. The application optimized tracking area lists across multiple network areas, reducing daily peak location registration signals by 65.3% and paging signals by 7.0%, lowering network signaling load and improving operational efficiency. In a representative problem spanning 333 base stations, it completed optimization in approximately five minutes, with results incorporated into DOCOMO’s network planning and day-to-day operations.
•Delivered Italy’s first annealing quantum computer, a 4,400+ qubit Advantage2™ system, for installation at Villa Saporiti in Como, Italy. Funded by Swiss Quantum Technology SA, the system will give European researchers, universities and businesses access to production-grade annealing quantum computing technology once installed, supporting Q-Alliance’s efforts to accelerate application development, build quantum workforce skills, and advance commercial adoption.
•Launched the gate-model quantum computing simulator beta program, advancing error-aware programming capabilities and marking an important milestone in D-Wave’s gate-model development roadmap. Built around D-Wave’s dual-rail superconducting gate-model technology, the simulator enables beta participants to experiment with error-aware programming and develop quantum applications in preparation for the Company’s forthcoming gate-model systems. The underlying technology is designed to harness error detection to
deliver efficient quantum error correction with significantly lower hardware overhead as systems scale. Beta participants include BBVA, FirstQFM, Florida Atlantic University and the Jülich Supercomputing Centre.
•Extended D-Wave’s longstanding partnership with SkyWater through a multi-year agreement for specialized semiconductor process development and manufacturing to support D-Wave’s quantum technology roadmap. Also completed a successful proof-of-technology project using D-Wave’s annealing quantum computing technology to optimize maintenance scheduling at SkyWater’s fab, reducing lot delays by up to 30% and improving factory throughput. The project will advance to a proof-of-concept incorporating resource availability, technician constraints, and additional fab-specific operational constraints.
•Formed a strategic partnership with CGI, one of the world’s largest independent IT and business consulting services firms. CGI plans to integrate D-Wave’s quantum computing platforms into its advanced technology services portfolio to help clients develop and deploy quantum and hybrid-quantum applications across industries, including logistics, transportation and retail.
•Signed a memorandum of understanding with LG CNS, a leading AI transformation company in South Korea, to advance the adoption of quantum computing for enterprise optimization in South Korea and beyond. The collaboration will focus on applying D-Wave’s annealing quantum computing technology to complex optimization challenges, with the goal of moving applications from proof-of-concept into production.
•Recognized as a Market Shaper in the Gartner® Emerging Market Quadrant for Quantum Computing - Established Vendors as of November 2026, and was among six quantum computing vendors in the upper right quadrant.*
•Strengthened D-Wave’s board expertise with the appointments of Bernard Gavgani and Kevan Krysler to the Board of Directors. Mr. Gavgani, former Group Chief Information Officer of BNP Paribas, joined the Cybersecurity Committee, bringing deep expertise in enterprise technology strategy. Mr. Krysler, a veteran technology finance executive, joined the Audit Committee, bringing extensive experience in public company financial reporting, corporate growth strategy, and risk management.

Third Quarter 2026 Financial Highlights
•Revenue: Revenue was $4.5 million, up 21% from the third quarter of 2025. Professional services revenue increased by $1.5 million, and quantum computing as a service (“QCaaS”) revenue increased by $0.8 million, partially offset by a decrease in system sales revenue of $1.5 million.
•Bookings1: Bookings were $1.9 million, a year over year decrease of approximately $0.4 million, or 19%. Bookings in any single quarter can vary significantly based on the timing of larger transactions. Bookings for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 were $37.4 million, an increase of $32.1 million, or 611%, from the nine months ended September 30, 2025.
•Remaining Performance Obligations (RPO)2: As of September 30, 2026, RPO totaled $38.3 million, an increase of 1,221%, from the $2.9 million as of September 30, 2025. Approximately 59% of the September 30, 2026 RPO balance is expected to be recognized as revenue within the following 12 months, and approximately 71% is expected to be recognized as revenue within the following 24 months.
•Customers: Recognized revenue from more than 100 customers, 48% of which were commercial enterprises.
•GAAP Gross Profit and Gross Margin: GAAP gross profit was $2.4 million, a year over year decrease of $0.3 million, or 11%. GAAP gross margin was 52.9%, a year over year decrease of 18.5%. The decrease in gross profit and gross margin was due primarily to a revenue mix with a higher proportion of professional services revenue that carries a lower margin, as well as an increase in non-cash stock-based compensation of $0.2 million.
•Non-GAAP Gross Profit and Gross Margin3: Non-GAAP Gross Profit was $2.9 million, essentially flat year over year. Non-GAAP Gross Margin was 63.6%, a year over year decrease of 14.1%, with the decrease due primarily to a revenue mix with a higher proportion of professional services revenue that carries a lower margin.
•GAAP Operating Expenses: GAAP operating expenses were $55.9 million, a year over year increase of $25.5 million, or 84%. The increase was driven primarily by increases of $12.3 million in personnel costs, $4.1 million in non-cash stock-based compensation, $4.6 million in non-cash depreciation and amortization, $1.1 million in third party professional services, and $1.1 million in marketing expenses.
•Non-GAAP Adjusted Operating Expenses3: Non-GAAP Adjusted Operating Expenses were $40.5 million, a year over year increase of $17.0 million, or 72%.
•Net Loss: Net loss was $48.2 million, or $0.13 per share, a year over year decrease of $91.8 million, or $0.28 per share. The decrease was primarily due to a $121.9 million decrease in the amount of non-cash, non-operating charges related to the remeasurement of the Company's warrant liability. All of the Company's remaining publicly traded warrants were redeemed in November 2025.
•Adjusted EBITDA Loss3: Adjusted EBITDA Loss was $37.7 million, a year over year increase of $17.0 million, or 83%, with the increase due primarily to increased operating expenses to support the Company’s accelerated product development and go-to-market initiatives.
•Balance Sheet and Liquidity: As of September 30, 2026, consolidated cash and marketable investment securities totaled $503.4 million. During the third quarter of 2026, the Company executed a definitive agreement with the U.S. Department of Commerce, providing it with access to up to $100.0 million of U.S. CHIPS and Science Act funding, subject to the achievement of specified milestones.

Non-GAAP Financial Measures
To supplement the financial information presented in accordance with GAAP, we use non-GAAP measures of certain components of financial performance. Each of Non-GAAP Gross Profit, Non-GAAP Gross Margin, Non-GAAP Adjusted Operating Expenses, and Adjusted EBITDA Loss is a financial measure that is not required by or presented in accordance with GAAP. Management believes that each measure provides investors an additional meaningful method to evaluate certain aspects of such results period over period. The Company defines each of its non-GAAP financial measures as follows:
•Non-GAAP Gross Profit is defined as GAAP gross profit without the effect of depreciation and amortization expense and non-cash stock-based compensation expense. We use Non-GAAP Gross Profit to measure, understand and evaluate our core operating performance and trends and to develop short-term and long-term operating plans.
•Non-GAAP Gross Margin is defined as GAAP gross margin adjusted to exclude depreciation and amortization expense and non-cash stock-based compensation expense. We use Non-GAAP Gross Margin to measure, understand and evaluate our core business performance.
•Non-GAAP Adjusted Operating Expenses is defined as operating expenses before depreciation and amortization expense, non-operating or non-recurring expenses and non-cash stock-based compensation expense. We use Non-GAAP Adjusted Operating Expenses to measure our operating expenses, excluding items we do not believe directly reflect our core operations.
•Adjusted EBITDA Loss is defined as net loss before interest income, tax provision, interest expense, depreciation and amortization expense, stock-based compensation, remeasurements of liability-classified warrants, gain on marketable equity securities and other non-operating or non-recurring income and expenses. We use Adjusted EBITDA Loss to measure the operating performance of our business, excluding specifically identified items that we do not believe directly reflect our core operations and may not be indicative of our recurring operations.
The presentation of non-GAAP financial measures is not meant to be considered in isolation or as a substitute for the financial results prepared in accordance with GAAP, and our presentation of non-GAAP measures may be different from non-GAAP measures used by other companies.
