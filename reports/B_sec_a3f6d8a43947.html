Barrick Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results

Fourth straight quarter of strong operational delivery

 

–

Gold production of 816 thousand ounces1, up 3% from Q2 2026, and 6% year-over-year (excluding divested assets).2

 

–

Strong operating performance across the portfolio: Cortez benefited from the continued Goldrush ramp-up, Carlin’s Gold Quarry roaster posted a record quarter, and Loulo-Gounkoto, in a strong ramp-up that remains ahead of schedule, produced 16% more than in Q2 2026.

 

–

Productivity improvements mitigated cost pressures from fuel, consumables, and local currency appreciation. Gold cost of sales3 was $1,950 per ounce, and AISC4 was $1,911 per ounce.

 

–

Revenue rose 22% year-over-year to $5.07 billion, while attributable adjusted EBITDA4 increased 22% to $2.47 billion.

 

–

Net earnings increased 1% year-over-year to $1.32 billion, or $0.80 per share, while adjusted net earnings per share4 increased 41% to $0.82 per share.

 

–

Full-year production guidance reiterated: 2.90–3.25 million ounces1 of gold, and 190,000–220,000 tonnes1 of copper.

 

–

Returned $1.04 billion to shareholders through $761 million of share repurchases and a quarterly dividend of $0.175 per share, driving a 23% year-over-year increase in total shareholder returns. The Company also strengthened its balance sheet, ending the quarter with net cash5 of $0.9 billion, up from $0.3 billion in Q3 2025.

All amounts expressed in U.S. dollars

TORONTO, November 9, 2026 – Barrick Mining Corporation (NYSE:B)(TSX:ABX) (“Barrick” or the “Company”) today reported third quarter operating and financial results for the period ended September 30, 2026. Barrick produced 816 thousand ounces1 of gold and 50 thousand tonnes1 of copper in the quarter. The Company generated $5.07 billion in revenue, $2.49 billion in operating cash flow, $1.80 billion in attributable operating cash flow4, and $0.64 billion in attributable free cash flow4. Net earnings per share for the quarter were $0.80, and adjusted net earnings per share4 were $0.82—up 5% and 41%, respectively, from Q3 2025.

“Twelve months ago, we set out to earn back trust and restore Barrick to its history of discipline and excellence,” said Mark Hill, President and CEO. “This quarter marks a full year of consistent delivery. For the fourth consecutive quarter, we improved safety, executed against our plans, strengthened Nevada Gold Mines, advanced our growth projects, and returned significant capital to shareholders. We remain on track to achieve our full-year production guidance, and we head into the year’s final months with momentum, drive, and discipline.”

 

 

 

 

     

 

Operational Highlights

Operational performance was strong across the portfolio, with record roaster throughput at Carlin’s Gold Quarry, continued Goldrush ramp-up at Cortez, and strong performances from Loulo-Gounkoto, Bulyanhulu, and Kibali. Gold production in the North America region was 497 thousand ounces.1 The portfolio that combines Barrick’s AME and South America & Asia Pacific regions—which we previously referred to as Rest of World, and which will henceforth be known as Barrick International—produced 319 thousand ounces1 of gold, and 50 thousand tonnes of copper. Barrick’s total attributable gold production was 2% lower than the prior-year period, largely due to the impacts of extreme weather at Veladero, and delayed access to high-grade material at North Mara, which is expected to be accessed in Q4 2026.

Gold cost of sales (“COS”)3 for Q3 2026 was $1,950 per ounce, compared to $1,562 per ounce in the prior-year period, primarily reflecting lower grades processed, higher royalties associated with increased realized gold prices4, and elevated diesel prices. Total cash costs (“TCC”)4 increased to $1,386 per ounce from $1,137 per ounce compared to Q3 2025, while all-in sustaining costs (“AISC”)4 increased to $1,911 per ounce from $1,538, primarily reflecting the same cost pressures, together with higher sustaining capital expenditures.4

Copper COS6, C1 cash costs4, and AISC4 increased to $3.65 per pound, $2.76 per pound, and $4.52 per pound, respectively, from Q3 2025, primarily reflecting higher diesel prices, and local currency appreciation, particularly in labor costs.

Financial Highlights

Continued operational delivery allowed Barrick to capture more of the strong metal prices and deliver another quarter of robust financial performance. Net earnings for the quarter were $1.32 billion, or $0.80 per share, while adjusted net earnings4 were $1.35 billion, or $0.82 per share, compared to net earnings of $1.30 billion, or $0.76 per share, and adjusted net earnings4 of $0.98 billion, or $0.58 per share, in the prior-year period.

Revenue increased 22% year-over-year to $5.07 billion, EBITDA4 increased 13% to $3.22 billion, and attributable adjusted EBITDA4 increased 22% to $2.47 billion. Operating cash flow totaled $2.49 billion during the quarter. Attributable operating cash flow4 was $1.80 billion, and attributable free cash flow4 was $0.64 billion.

Key Growth Projects

Nevada Gold Mines. At Cortez, we are advancing drilling along one of the most prospective corridors in Nevada. At Carlin, we are focused on drilling out greater Leeville and optimizing processing capacity. We are also assessing opportunities to optimize infrastructure across Nevada Gold Mines. Open pit growth at Carlin has the potential to extend open pit mine life beyond 2050.7

Fourmile continues to show its potential to be a Tier One Gold Asset.8 Advanced studies are underway incorporating drilling, metallurgical test work, geotechnical investigations, and mine design. Infill and resource definition drilling remains a key priority, while exploration efforts continue to unlock additional value across the project area.

Lumwana (Zambia). The mill expansion is progressing well and is expected to double copper production. The project remains on budget and on schedule for first copper at the end of Q1 20289, and we expect 2026 capital spending to come in at the lower end of guidance. All major long-lead equipment has been procured, and the mill shells and trunnions, primary crusher, and tailings thickener are already on site. The primary crusher’s third level is nearly complete, the tailings storage facility expansion contract has been awarded and work is expected to commence in Q4 2026, and the last mill wall lifts are expected to be completed in Q4 2026.

Pueblo Viejo (Dominican Republic). The expansion is advancing, with the Naranjo tailings storage facility as the main focus. Temporary water management structures are over 20% complete, and detailed design work remains on schedule to support submission of the full starter dam permit package in Q1 2027. Two of the four bridges on Haul Roads 17 and 19 are under construction, and the flotation improvement work is progressing through the prefeasibility stage. Resettlement is nearly complete, with 99% of packages accepted.

Returns to Shareholders

A quarterly dividend of $0.175 per share has been declared in respect of performance for the third quarter of 2026. The Q3 2026 dividend will be paid on December 15, 2026, to shareholders of record at the close of business on November 30, 2026.

Barrick’s dividend policy targets a total payout of 50% of attributable free cash flow4 on an annualized basis through a fixed quarterly dividend of $0.175 per share, and a year-end performance top-up based on attributable free cash flow4 generated during the year. Actual distributions may vary from the target level depending on cash flow, capital requirements, balance sheet considerations, and other factors.

In addition to the quarterly dividend, Barrick repurchased $761 million of shares during the quarter under its previously announced $3.0 billion share buyback program. The repurchases reflect the Company’s commitment to returning cash to shareholders, and its continued confidence in the value of its assets and long-term growth prospects. The repurchase authorization does not obligate the Company to acquire common shares.

2026 Guidance

Barrick continues to expect 2026 gold production to be 2.90–3.25 million ounces1. Gold cost guidance for 2026 remains unchanged, including COS3 of $1,870–$2,070 per ounce, TCC4 of $1,330–$1,470 per ounce, and AISC4 of $1,760–$1,950 per ounce, based on a gold price assumption of $4,500 per ounce.

Copper production guidance for 2026 remains unchanged at 190,000–220,000 tonnes1. Copper cost guidance remains unchanged at COS6 of $3.05–$3.35 per pound, C1 cash costs4 of $2.20–$2.45 per pound, and AISC4 of $3.45–$3.75 per pound, based on a copper price assumption of $5.50 per pound.

Guidance is based on an oil price (WTI) assumption of $70 per barrel. For every $10 per barrel change in the oil price, the direct impact on costs associated with diesel consumption is approximately $12 per ounce across Barrick’s gold operations, and $0.04 per pound across its copper operations.

Endnote 4 – Non-GAAP Financial Measures

Total cash costs per ounce and All-in sustaining costs per ounce

“Total cash costs” per ounce (TCC/oz) and “All-in sustaining costs” per ounce (AISC/oz) are non-GAAP financial measures which are calculated based on the definition published by the World Gold Council (a market development organization for the gold industry comprised of and funded by gold mining companies from around the world, including Barrick, the “WGC”). The WGC is not a regulatory organization. Management uses these measures to monitor the performance of our gold mining operations and their ability to generate positive cash flow, both on an individual site basis and an overall gold operations basis. TCC/oz starts with our cost of sales related to gold production and removes depreciation, the non-controlling interest of cost of sales and costs allocated to by-products. AISC/oz starts with TCC/oz and includes sustaining capital expenditures, sustaining leases, general and administrative costs, minesite exploration and evaluation costs related to the current mine plan and reclamation cost accretion and amortization. Barrick believes that the use of TCC/oz and AISC/oz will assist analysts, investors and other stakeholders of Barrick in understanding the costs associated with producing gold, understanding the economics of gold mining, assessing our operating performance and also our ability to generate free cash flow from the gold operations portion of our business. Due to the capital-intensive nature of the industry and the long useful lives over which these items are depreciated, there can be a significant timing difference between net earnings calculated in accordance with IFRS and the amount of free cash flow that is generated by a mine and therefore Barrick believes these measures are useful non-GAAP operating metrics and supplement our IFRS disclosures. These measures are not representative of all of Barrick’s cash expenditures as they do not include income tax payments, interest costs or dividend payments. These measures do not include depreciation or amortization. Realized Price

“Realized price” is a non-GAAP financial measure which excludes from sales: treatment and refining charges; and cumulative catch-up adjustment to revenue relating to our streaming arrangements. Barrick believes this provides investors and analysts with a more accurate measure with which to compare to market gold and copper prices and to assess our gold and copper sales performance. For those reasons, management believes that this measure provides a more accurate reflection of our Company’s past performance and is a better indicator of its expected performance in future periods. The realized price measure is intended to provide additional information, and does not have any standardized definition under IFRS and should not be considered in isolation or as a substitute for measures of performance prepared in accordance with IFRS. The measure is not necessarily indicative of sales as determined under IFRS. Other companies may calculate this measure differently. Further details on these non-GAAP financial measures are provided in the MD&A accompanying Barrick’s financial statements filed from time to time on SEDAR+ at www.sedarplus.ca and on EDGAR at www.sec.gov.
