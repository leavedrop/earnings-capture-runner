Archer Announces Q3 2026 Results; Receives FAA Market Survey Certificate Unlocking LA Operations and Boeing Deal Close Imminent

Nov 9, 2026
  • Download (opens in new window)
  • Received Midnight’s Market Survey Special Airworthiness Certificate from the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) unlocking operations in Los Angeles in coming weeks as Archer prepares for carrying passengers as part of Department of Transportation’s eVTOL Integration Pilot Program (eIPP) and LA28 Games.
  • Acquisition of Boeing’s Wisk, Insitu, and SkyGrid businesses nearing close, set to accelerate Archer’s evolution into becoming a diversified aerospace and defense platform with significant revenue generating operations1.
  • Began testing the turboshaft engine for Archer’s Halo/Thunder hybrid, autonomous VTOL platform and remain on-track to begin full-scale flight testing next year with Anduril.

  • Archer partners with Formula 1 on a multi-year relationship to explore the future integration of eVTOL air taxi services at Grand Prix events.

  • Strong financial performance for the quarter with Adjusted EBITDA in line with guidance and $1.4B in liquidity2.

SANTA CLARA, Calif.--(BUSINESS WIRE)-- Archer Aviation Inc. (“Archer” or the “Company”) (NYSE: ACHR) today announced operating and financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.

Midnight flying over the San Francisco Bay in October as part of ‘No Roads’ tour

Commenting on third quarter 2026 results, Adam Goldstein said:

“I started Archer to unlock the skies, and we are now doing that across both commercial and defense. With our first aircraft in Los Angeles and Boeing set to become our largest shareholder following our transaction close, I believe we are building an end-to-end aerospace and defense platform that very few companies could assemble, and one that will matter for decades.”

Shareholder Letter

The Company issued a letter to shareholders from founder and CEO, Adam Goldstein, discussing highlights from the quarter.

Earnings Call

If you’d like to join Archer’s earnings call, it will be hosted live at 2:00 p.m. Pacific Time today and can be accessed via Archer’s investor relations website at investors.archer.com. After the call, a replay and transcript will be available there.

Q3’26 Highlights

Received Midnight’s Market Survey Certificate from FAA unlocking operations in Los Angeles in coming weeks as Archer prepares for carrying passengers as part of eIPP and LA28 Games

Archer’s Midnight aircraft recently received its Market Survey Special Airworthiness Certificate from the FAA unlocking piloted operations in Los Angeles in preparation for carrying passengers as part of the eIPP and LA28 Olympic Games. This announcement comes on the heels of Archer completing the Northern California portion of its ‘No Roads’ flight tour in October, which included over 300 miles of piloted flights across the Bay Area and culminated in multiple flights around the San Francisco Bay in and out of Oakland International Airport. These city-to-city flights showcased Midnight’s ability to integrate seamlessly into complex air space shared by three major international airports.

Acquisition of Boeing’s Wisk, Insitu, and SkyGrid businesses nearing close, set to accelerate Archer’s evolution into becoming a diversified aerospace and defense platform with significant revenue generating operations1

The goal of Archer’s acquisitions is to create an end-to-end aerospace and defense platform, combining Wisk, Insitu and SkyGrid’s pioneering autonomy and airspace intelligence technologies with Archer’s Midnight air taxi and Halo/Thunder UAS platform. Insitu alone will add over $200M in annual revenue to Archer’s business with operations across 35 countries3. As part of the transaction, Boeing is set to become a strategic partner and Archer’s largest shareholder, establishing an ongoing Archer and Boeing collaboration and technology sharing arrangement.

Began testing turboshaft engine for Halo/Thunder hybrid, autonomous VTOL platform; Full-Scale Flight Testing On Track for 2027

Archer began testing the turboshaft engine for its Halo/Thunder autonomous, hybrid-electric VTOL platform it is developing with Anduril. The turbo-generator is one of the central new technologies in the platform, extending Archer’s flight-proven electric powertrain into a new class of range, speed and payload. The same airframe, powertrain and core systems will support both the Halo commercial variant and the Thunder defense variant. The program remains on track to begin full-scale flight testing next year.

Archer partners with Formula 1

Archer and Formula 1 are forming a strategic alliance through a multi-year relationship to explore the future integration of eVTOL air taxi services at Grand Prix events with the goal to fly VIPs and fans to and from Grand Prix circuits in minutes aboard Midnight.

Strong financial performance for the quarter

Revenue was $5.1 million. Net Loss was $274.6 million and Adjusted EBITDA was a loss of $178.5 million, at the favorable end of the Company’s guided range of $170 million to $200 million. Archer ended the quarter with $1.4 billion in cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments, providing the Company a continued strong foundation.

Key Financial Highlights

Liquidity & Cash Flows

  • We ended Q3 2026 with $1,353.6 million of cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments on our balance sheet and an additional $18.4 million of restricted cash.
  • Our Q3 2026 cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments decreased by $207 million from Q2 2026, primarily due to the $164.6 million cash used in operating activities, $31.7 million used in the purchase of property and equipment and $10.0 million used for an investment in a private company.

Revenue

  • Our Q3 2026 Revenue was $5.1 million compared to $5.0 million in Q2 2026. We delivered our first engines for Omen to Anduril in Q3 2026.

Operating Expenses & Net Loss

  • Q3 2026 Operating Expenses increased by $10.1 million from Q2 2026 as we continued to invest in expanding flight testing, certification efforts, and production activities for our Midnight aircraft, along with the investment in the design and development efforts for our hybrid aircraft, and the development of our AI products, with research and development expenses increasing by $12.3 million, partially offset by a $2.9 million decrease in general and administrative expenses.
  • Q3 2026 Net Loss increased by $11.4 million from Q2 2026 primarily driven by $10.1 million increase in operating expenses and a $1.6 million decrease in interest income, net.

Adjusted EBITDA

  • Q3 2026 Adjusted EBITDA was a loss of $178.5 million, which is on the favorable end of the guidance range of $170 million - $200 million. The loss was an increase of $1.4 million over Q2 2026, mainly due to the reasons mentioned above for the increase in operating expenses.

Q4 2026 Financial Estimates

  • Archer’s financial estimates for the fourth quarter of 2026 are as follows:
    • Revenue is expected to be $5 million to $6 million
    • Adjusted EBITDA expected to be a loss of $185 million to $205 million.

We have not reconciled our Adjusted EBITDA estimates because certain items that impact non-GAAP metrics are uncertain or out of our control and cannot be reasonably predicted. In particular, stock-based compensation expense and change in fair value of warrants is impacted by the future fair market value of our common stock and warrants along with other factors, all of which are difficult to predict, subject to frequent change, or not within our control. The actual amount of these expenses during the fourth quarter of 2026 will have a significant impact on our future GAAP financial results. Accordingly, a reconciliation of non-GAAP metrics is not available without unreasonable effort.

Non-GAAP Financial Measures

To supplement our consolidated financial results prepared in accordance with GAAP, we use the following non-GAAP financial measures: Non-GAAP total operating expenses and Adjusted EBITDA. The presentation of these financial measures is not intended to be considered in isolation or as a substitute for, or superior to, financial information prepared and presented in accordance with GAAP. Investors are cautioned that there are material limitations associated with the use of non-GAAP financial measures as an analytical tool. In particular, these non-GAAP financial measures have no standardized meaning prescribed by GAAP and are not based on a comprehensive set of accounting rules or principles and many of the adjustments to the GAAP financial measures reflect the exclusion of items that are recurring and may be reflected in our financial results for the foreseeable future. In addition, these measures may be different from non-GAAP financial measures used by other companies, limiting their usefulness for comparison purposes. We compensate for these limitations by providing specific information regarding the GAAP amounts excluded from these non-GAAP financial measures.

We believe that the use of non-GAAP financial measures helps us evaluate our business and financial performance, identify trends impacting our business, formulate business plans and financial projections, and make strategic decisions. We believe that disclosing non-GAAP financial measures to the readers of our financial statements provides useful supplemental data that, while not a substitute for GAAP financial measures, can offer insight in the review of our operating and financial results and enables investors to more fully understand our performance and cash trends by removing the effects of certain non-cash expenses and non-recurring items.

We excluded items in the following general categories from one or more of our non-GAAP financial measures, certain of which are described below:

Stock-Based Compensation Expense

We exclude stock-based compensation expense, which is a non-cash expense, from these non-GAAP financial measures because we believe that excluding this item provides meaningful supplemental information on our operating results and enhances our ability and the ability of investors to understand the impact of non-cash stock-based compensation expense on our operating results.

Warrant Expense and Gains or Losses from Revaluation of Warrants

Expense from our common stock warrants issued to Stellantis, which is recurring (but non-cash), expense from one-time issuance of warrants and gains or losses from change in fair value of warrants from revaluation will be reflected in our financial results for the foreseeable future. We exclude warrant expenses and gains or losses from change in fair value for similar reasons to our stock-based compensation expense.

Litigation Related-Expense

We exclude one-time accrued cash expenses incurred related to litigation settlement, from these non-GAAP financial measures because we believe these transaction-specific expenses are inconsistent in amount and frequency and do not correlate to the operation of our business and excluding these provides meaningful supplemental information on our operating results and enhances our ability and the ability of the investors to understand the impact of non-recurring litigation-related expense on our operating results.

Acquisition-Related Expense

We exclude cash expenses, including diligence, legal, advisory and other costs incurred with acquisitions, from these non-GAAP financial measures because we believe these transaction-specific expenses are inconsistent in amount and frequency and do not correlate to the operation of our business and excluding these provides meaningful supplemental information on our operating results and enhances our ability and the ability of the investors to understand the impact of non-recurring acquisition-related expense on our operating results.
