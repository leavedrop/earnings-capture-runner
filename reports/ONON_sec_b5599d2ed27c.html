Overview
On is a premium performance sportswear brand rooted in innovation, design, and sustainability. Since our founding in the Swiss Alps in 2010, we have built a distinctive global brand with a passionate community across more than 90 countries. Through our premium product and brand experience, we bring our mission—to ignite the human spirit through movement—to life for our fans worldwide.
We believe our premium positioning and our relentless focus on performance and design sets us apart within the global sportswear market. Our culture of innovation has enabled us to repeatedly introduce groundbreaking technologies designed to elevate the running experience and create enduring excitement around our brand. Anchored in our running heritage, we have extended this expertise into other performance categories, including performance outdoor, performance all-day, performance tennis, and performance training, connecting us with new communities across a full spectrum of movement.
On operates as a single-brand consumer products business and therefore has a single operating and reportable segment.

The increase in gross profit margin was mainly driven by CHF 52.6 million of U.S. tariff refunds received in the three-month period ended September 30, 2026, which increased the gross profit margin by 570 basis points. These increases were partially offset by the non-recurrence of a one-off favorable impact of approximately 200 basis points from lower-than-anticipated freight and other costs during the prior year comparison period ended September 30, 2025.

The increase was primarily driven by additional expenses incurred as a result of our expanding retail footprint, primarily due to retail store-related personnel costs and depreciation.
•Marketing expenses as a percentage of net sales decreased to 12.1% during the three-month period ended September 30, 2026 compared to 12.3% during the three-month period ended September 30, 2025. The decrease was primarily driven by a shift in the timing of our marketing campaigns as well as strong net sales growth.

Nine-month period ended September 30, 2026 compared to the nine-month period ended September 30, 2025
SG&A expenses for the nine-month period ended September 30, 2026 increased by CHF 210.3 million, or 18.7%, to CHF 1,334.1 million, compared to CHF 1,123.8 million for the nine-month period ended September 30, 2025. The drivers for the fluctuations in SG&A expenses, mostly denominated as a percentage of net sales, can be summarized as follows:
•Distribution expenses as a percentage of net sales decreased to 10.2% during the nine-month period ended September 30, 2026 compared to 10.9% during the nine-month period ended September 30, 2025. Customs and duty expenses
Beginning in April 2025, the United States ("U.S") implemented new tariffs as part of its trade policy, including both baseline tariffs and higher country-specific reciprocal tariffs on imports from all countries. Additionally, on July 31, 2025, an executive order ("Executive Order") was signed confirming the tariff rates on various trading partner countries, including a fixed reciprocal import tariff of 20% on Vietnam (applied in addition to the existing 20% import tariff in Vietnam). The confirmed tariff rates under the Executive Order became effective in August, 2025. In July 2026, the U.S. government imposed additional tariffs under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 that are expected to result in an increase in tariffs on our products.
On February 20, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court invalidated specific tariffs levied under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (“IEEPA”), and the U.S government ceased collecting these tariffs on February 24, 2026. On April 20, 2026, the U.S. Customs and Border Protection (“CBP”) introduced an electronic system to manage refunds for these IEEPA tariffs.
As of September 30, 2026, our total IEEPA tariffs paid are approximately CHF 57.4 million. Of this amount, approximately CHF 52.6 million have been submitted through the U.S. Customs refund processing and received during the nine-months ended September 30, 2026. These amounts relate to inventories already sold. As such, we recognized these amounts as a reduction to cost of sales within our consolidated financial statements during the nine-months ended September 30, 2026, consistent with our previous disclosures.
Subsequent to September 30, 2026, we submitted further claims for IEEPA tariff refunds of approximately CHF 4.5 million, all of which had been accepted by the CBP prior to November 10, 2026. We determined that recovery of these amounts was not virtually certain as of September 30, 2026, and therefore have not recognized them in the unaudited interim condensed consolidated financial statements. We will recognize these refunds when their realization is deemed virtually certain, as a reduction to cost of sales, as they are attributable to inventories already sold.
We will continue to monitor changes to the import and export policies of the U.S. and other countries, including the imposition of additional tariffs, that could impact our financial position, results of operations and cash flows.

Macroeconomic conditions
Global events, including the recent military conflict in the Middle East, have resulted in worldwide geopolitical and macroeconomic uncertainty, and we cannot predict how these conflicts will evolve or the timing and effects thereof. Continued instability could adversely affect consumer demand and our sales and growth in those markets. In addition, the conflict could further disrupt regional trade routes. We will continue to monitor the situation and the potential impact it may have on consumer patterns and sentiment in the Middle East and other regions.
We are subject to risks and exposures from the evolving global macroeconomic environment, including the level of promotional activity in the market and economic uncertainty, all of which may negatively impact customer demand, consumer spending, our ability to manage inventory, and our sales and growth. In the second and third quarters of 2026, growth in our wholesale channel was more moderate given selective sell-in actions with partners, particularly in the Americas, as we continue to operate in a highly promotional market / industry. These actions are a result of our commitment to our premium strategy and disciplined execution, including selective actions taken in the fourth quarter of 2026 and the potential for further action to manage sell-in for the remainder of the year.

We use adjusted net income, adjusted basic EPS and adjusted diluted EPS as measures of operating performance in conjunction with related IFRS measures.
